Unitarity of CFT

Unitarity of CFT

Jan 18, 20265 min read

  • check

In CFT, we are working on infinite dimensional representations of the conformal group, as this group is not compact and therefore can’t admit finite unitary representations.
Working in euclidian signature SO(d+1,1)SO(d+1,1)SO(d+1,1), the claim is that highest weight representations in this signature become unitary representations of SO(d,2)SO(d,2)SO(d,2). Note that they can’t both be unitary. Think of it this way:

tE=−itMt_E = -it_MtE​=−itM​

in order to preserve rotation invariance in scalars, the rotation needs to change accordingly. The unitarity in Minkowski signature is guaranteed by the Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction theorem, reported here too:

Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction theorem

Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction theorem

If we have a Minkowski theory which is an analytical continuation of a euclidian one:

Euclidian th. ⟷analytical cont. Minkowski th.\textrm{Euclidian th. }\overset{\textrm{analytical cont.}}{\longleftrightarrow}\textrm{ Minkowski th.}Euclidian th. ⟷analytical cont.​ Minkowski th.

Then, the Minkowski theory is unitary if the euclidian one is reflection positive

Link to original

We define a reflection Θ\ThetaΘ in our euclidian theory in the cylindrical picture.
We select the reflection to be the τ\tauτ inversion:

In the corresponding radial picture, we are sending r↦1/rr\mapsto 1/rr↦1/r.
So, in order to have a unitary theory in Minkowski, we need that, in Euclidean signature, the following is satisfied:

⟨I(ψ),ψ⟩>0∀ψ\corr{I(\psi),\psi}>0\quad \forall \psi⟨I(ψ),ψ⟩>0∀ψ

How do we verify that this condition is consistent with conformal algebra? Take a primary state ∣ψP⟩\ket{\psi _P}∣ψP​⟩, then:

  • Assume that ⟨I(ψP),ψP⟩>0\corr{I(\psi_P ),\psi_P}>0⟨I(ψP​),ψP​⟩>0
  • We verify that the first descendant satisfies the condition
⟨I(PμψP),PνψP⟩≽0\corr{I(P_\mu\psi_P ),P_\nu\psi_P}\succcurlyeq 0⟨I(Pμ​ψP​),Pν​ψP​⟩≽0

here the condition is in the sense of positive semi-definiteness. This tool can be used to impose bounds on the dimensionality of primaries.

Info

We are basically redefining the dual states. In normal quantum mechanics, those are the ‘dagger’ states, while here we need to perform the radial inversion.

Representation of conformal group in 1D

Here, the conformal group basically reduces to the group of linear transformations.
