Working in radial quantization, we want to understand how operators and states are related in conformally symmetric theories.
The main result we want to prove is the following:

State-operator correspondence

In CFT, states are defined on the surface of a sphere and are generated by operators inside the sphere corresponding to the state. The statement can be summarized by:

θ(0)↔θ(0)∣0⟩≔∣θ⟩\theta (0) \leftrightarrow \theta(0)\ket{0}\coloneqq \ket{\theta}θ(0)↔θ(0)∣0⟩:=∣θ⟩

State ← operator

Performing the path integral on an empty sphere produces the vacuum state ∣0⟩\ket{0}∣0⟩.
On the surface of the sphere, we have a Hilbert space spanned by the states ∣ϕ∂B⟩\ket{\phi _{\partial B}}∣ϕ∂B​⟩ which correspond to a field configuration ϕ∂B(n)\phi _{\partial B}(\mathbf{n})ϕ∂B​(n) on ∂B{\partial B}∂B, so that a generic state ∣ψ⟩\ket{\psi}∣ψ⟩ can be written as:

∫Dϕ∂B⟨ϕ∂B|ψ⟩∣ϕ∂B⟩\int\mathcal{D}\phi _{\partial B} \ip{\phi _{\partial B}}{\psi}\ket{\phi _{\partial B}}∫Dϕ∂B​⟨ϕ∂B​∣ψ⟩∣ϕ∂B​⟩

The coefficients ⟨ϕ∂B|ψ⟩\ip{\phi _{\partial B}}{\psi}⟨ϕ∂B​∣ψ⟩ are given by the path integral over the interior of the sphere BBB. Consider the case ∣ψ⟩=∣0⟩\ket{\psi}=\ket{0}∣ψ⟩=∣0⟩ and assume RRR to be the radius of BBB. Then:

⟨ϕ∂B|0⟩=∫r<Rϕ(R,n)=ϕ∂B(n)Dϕ(r,n)e−S[ϕ]\ip{\phi _{\partial B}}{0} = \int_{\substack{r<R\\ \phi (R,\mathbf{n}) = \phi_{\partial B}(\mathbf{n})}}\mathcal{D}\phi (r,\mathbf{n})e^{-S[\phi]}⟨ϕ∂B​∣0⟩=∫r<Rϕ(R,n)=ϕ∂B​(n)​​Dϕ(r,n)e−S[ϕ]

We can perform the same steps inserting an operator θ(x)\theta (x)θ(x), where xxx can be anywhere inside the sphere:

θ(x)⟨ϕ∂B|θ(x)|0⟩=∫r<Rϕ(R,n)=ϕ∂B(n)Dϕ(r,n)θ(x)e−S[ϕ]\matel{\phi _{\partial B}}{\theta(x)}{0} = \int_{\substack{r<R\\ \phi (R,\mathbf{n}) = \phi_{\partial B}(\mathbf{n})}}\mathcal{D}\phi (r,\mathbf{n})\theta(x)e^{-S[\phi]}θ(x)⟨ϕ∂B​∣θ(x)∣0⟩=∫r<Rϕ(R,n)=ϕ∂B​(n)​​Dϕ(r,n)θ(x)e−S[ϕ]

This defines the state θ(x)∣0⟩{\theta(x)}\ket{0}θ(x)∣0⟩. We conclude that operators inside the sphere generate states on the surface of the sphere.

State → operator


Take ∣θi⟩\ket{\theta_i}∣θi​⟩ eigenstates of the dilation operator DDD defined on the surfaces ∂Bi\partial B_i∂Bi​ of the spheres BiB_iBi​.
We can convert these states into operators by “cutting holes” corresponding to the BiB_iBi​ and “glueing in” the states on the borders. This generates the same correlator we would obtain by considering operators inside the spheres:

⟨...θi(xi)...⟩=∫Dϕ∂Bi⟨ϕ∂Bi|θi⟩∫ϕ(x∈∂Bi)=ϕ∂Bix∉BiDϕ(x)e−S[ϕ]\corr{...\theta_i(x_i)...} = \int \mathcal{D}\phi _{\partial B_i}\ip{\phi_{\partial B_i}}{\theta _i}\int_{\substack{\phi(x\in \partial B_i) = \phi _{\partial B_i}\\x\notin B_i}} \mathcal{D} \phi (x)e^{-S[{\phi}]}⟨...θi​(xi​)...⟩=∫Dϕ∂Bi​​⟨ϕ∂Bi​​∣θi​⟩∫ϕ(x∈∂Bi​)=ϕ∂Bi​​x∈/Bi​​​Dϕ(x)e−S[ϕ]

This implies that states on the surfaces of the spheres correspond to operators inside the spheres.

State ←> operator

We define a local operator as an eigenstate of DDD in radial quantization. This provides us with the correspondence:

O(0)↔O(0)∣0⟩≔∣O⟩\mathcal{O}(0) \leftrightarrow \mathcal{O}(0)\ket{0}\coloneqq \ket{\mathcal{O}}O(0)↔O(0)∣0⟩:=∣O⟩
