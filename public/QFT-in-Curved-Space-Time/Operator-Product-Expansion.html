Operator Product Expansion

Jan 18, 20262 min read


The Operator Product Expansion (OPE) is a technique used in CFT inside a correlator to expand the product of two operators into a convergent sum of local operators applied to primaries.

O1I1(x1)×OPEO2I2(x2)∼inside corr.∑Ok primariesC12;JI1I2(X12,X23,∂3)OkJ(x3)O ^{I_1} _1(x_1)\overset{OPE}{\times}O^{I_2}_2(x_2)\overset{\textrm{inside corr.}}{\sim} \sum_{O_k \textrm{ primaries}}C ^{I_1 I_2}_{12;J}(X_{12},X_{23},\partial_3)O_k ^J(x_3)O1I1​​(x1​)×OPE​O2I2​​(x2​)∼inside corr.Ok​ primaries∑​C12;JI1​I2​​(X12​,X23​,∂3​)OkJ​(x3​)

Or, more conveniently for computations:

O1I1(x1)×OPEO2I2(x2)∼inside corr.∑Ok primariesC12;JI1I2(X12,∂2)OkJ(x2)O ^{I_1} _1(x_1)\overset{OPE}{\times}O^{I_2}_2(x_2)\overset{\textrm{inside corr.}}{\sim} \sum_{O_k \textrm{ primaries}}C ^{I_1 I_2}_{12;J}(X_{12},\partial_2)O_k ^J(x_2)O1I1​​(x1​)×OPE​O2I2​​(x2​)∼inside corr.Ok​ primaries∑​C12;JI1​I2​​(X12​,∂2​)OkJ​(x2​)

The first equation is important as it shows that it is possible to perform OPE every time there exists a ball encapsulating x1,x2x_1,x_2x1​,x2​ and separates the corresponding operators from all the others.
Using the state-operator correspondence, this can be translated into:

∣θ1θ2⟩=∑∣ϕ⟩∈HCθ1θ2ϕ(x)∣ϕ⟩=∑primaries θk∑desc nC(x)θ1,θ2θkμ1,...μn;IJPμ1...Pμn∣θk⟩\ket{\theta_1 \theta_2} = \sum _{\ket{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}}C_{\theta_1\theta_2\phi}(x)\ket{\phi} = \sum _{\textrm{primaries }\theta_k}\sum _{\textrm{desc } n}C(x)^{\mu_1,...\mu_n;IJ}_{\theta_1,\theta_2\theta _k}P_{\mu_1}...P_{\mu_n}\ket{\theta_k}∣θ1​θ2​⟩=∣ϕ⟩∈H∑​Cθ1​θ2​ϕ​(x)∣ϕ⟩=primaries θk​∑​desc n∑​C(x)θ1​,θ2​θk​μ1​,...μn​;IJ​Pμ1​​...Pμn​​∣θk​⟩

Properties

  • Convergent with finite radius
  • Valid inside path integral
  • The operator C is completely determined in CFTs up to some constant (see below)
  • An operator OOO appears in the OPE of ϕ(x1)ϕ(x2)\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)ϕ(x1​)ϕ(x2​) if and only if their 3PF is non zero:
O(y)⊂OPE[ϕ(x1)ϕ(x2)]⇔⟨O(y)ϕ(x1)ϕ(x2)⟩≠0O(y)\subset\mathrm{OPE}\left[\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\right]\Leftrightarrow\corr{O(y)\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)}\neq 0O(y)⊂OPE[ϕ(x1​)ϕ(x2​)]⇔⟨O(y)ϕ(x1​)ϕ(x2​)⟩=0
  • Only TST’s with even spin can have a non zero 3PF with two scalars:
⟨ϕϕOμ1...μl⟩≠0⇔O is TST and l is even\corr{\phi\phi O^{\mu_1...\mu_l}}\neq 0 \Leftrightarrow O \textrm{ is TST and } l\textrm{ is even}⟨ϕϕOμ1​...μl​⟩=0⇔O is TST and l is even

Example

Consider the 3PF:

⟨Φ1(P1)Φ2(P2)Φ3(P3)⟩=λΦ1Φ2Φ3X122h123X232h231X132h132\langle \Phi _1(P_1)\Phi _2(P_2)\Phi _3(P_3)\rangle =\frac{\lambda_{\Phi_1\Phi_2\Phi_3}}{X_{12}^{2h_{123}}X_{23}^{2h_{231}}X_{13}^{2h_{132}}}⟨Φ1​(P1​)Φ2​(P2​)Φ3​(P3​)⟩=X122h123​​X232h231​​X132h132​​λΦ1​Φ2​Φ3​​​

We can take the OPE on the first two operators

ϕ1(P1)ϕ2(P2)∼∑ϕkCϕ1,ϕ2ϕk(X12,∂2)ϕk(x2)\phi _1(P_1)\phi _2(P_2)\sim\sum_{\phi_k}C ^{\phi_k}_{\phi _1,\phi _2}(X_{12},\partial _2)\phi_k (x_2)ϕ1​(P1​)ϕ2​(P2​)∼ϕk​∑​Cϕ1​,ϕ2​ϕk​​(X12​,∂2​)ϕk​(x2​)

So that:

⟨ϕ1(x1)ϕ2(x2)ϕ3(x3)⟩=∑θkCϕ1,ϕ2θk(X12,∂2)⟨θk(x2)ϕ3(x3)⟩\begin{split} \langle \phi _1(x_1)\phi _2(x_2)\phi _3(x_3)\rangle &=\sum_{\theta_k}C ^{\theta_k}_{\phi _1,\phi _2}(X_{12},\partial _2)\langle \theta_k (x_2)\phi _3(x_3)\rangle \end{split}⟨ϕ1​(x1​)ϕ2​(x2​)ϕ3​(x3​)⟩​=θk​∑​Cϕ1​,ϕ2​θk​​(X12​,∂2​)⟨θk​(x2​)ϕ3​(x3​)⟩​

Since 2PF’s are diagonal:

⟨ϕ1(x1)ϕ2(x2)ϕ3(x3)⟩=∑θkCϕ1,ϕ2θk(X12,∂2)⟨θk(x2)ϕ3(x3)⟩=Cϕ1,ϕ2ϕ3(X12,∂2)1X232Δ3\begin{split} \langle \phi _1(x_1)\phi _2(x_2)\phi _3(x_3)\rangle &=\sum_{\theta_k}C ^{\theta_k}_{\phi _1,\phi _2}(X_{12},\partial _2)\langle \theta_k (x_2)\phi _3(x_3)\rangle\\ & =C ^{\phi_3}_{\phi _1,\phi _2}(X_{12},\partial _2)\frac{1}{X_{23}^{2\Delta _3}} \end{split}⟨ϕ1​(x1​)ϕ2​(x2​)ϕ3​(x3​)⟩​=θk​∑​Cϕ1​,ϕ2​θk​​(X12​,∂2​)⟨θk​(x2​)ϕ3​(x3​)⟩=Cϕ1​,ϕ2​ϕ3​​(X12​,∂2​)X232Δ3​​1​​

The coefficient C can be found by expanding for X12<<X13,X23X_{12}<<X_{13},X_{23}X12​<<X13​,X23​ and matching powers. We have done it to second order in OPE for 3PF.
